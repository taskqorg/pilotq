# Trusted published closing findings (#331)

Task [#331](https://github.com/taskqorg/pilotq/issues/331), “Resolve trusted published results in closed Telegram notifications,” requires accepted findings and limits without weakening source, route or revision guards (§5, R8).

## Question

Can PilotQ resolve referenced publish verdicts using installed TaskQ truth without implementing another TaskQ lifecycle fold?

## Findings

### Owning source and reproduction

Diagnosis preceded implementation. The supplied ignored installed-0.2.39 observation records a closed notification with unconfirmed-result text even though its TaskQ close references a publish containing Changed, Checks and Open. Private content and identifiers remain outside this report. This is attributed installed evidence, not a worker browser qualification.

`closing_detail` previously extracted findings only from the literal close body. TaskQ v4.6.58 §2 permits `Repeats: publish <id>` instead of copying a verdict. Installed `issue`, `inbox --json` and `status --closed --task N --json` readbacks establish that the closed-status conversation verdict already resolves that reference. Therefore PilotQ owns the missing consumption path; TaskQ need not expose a new API.

An invented `closed_publish_331` fixture failed before the fix with `referenced accepted findings`. After the change, the same case reaches the accepted findings through the canonical closed-status verdict. The final test also exercises unknown outcomes for edited/untrusted sources, absent reference identity, invalid attempts, foreign task/URL, changed revisions and a different canonical verdict. Comment array order does not select a result. Full close bodies and appended delivery receipts remain supported.

### Change and retained protection

The registered TaskQ CLI reads the inbox, issue and one task's closed status, then rereads inbox and issue. Changed readback remains unknown. The close must match the current closed inbox revision. A referenced publication must have the exact referenced earlier comment ID, trusted unedited author evidence and a valid publish header. Its verdict must equal TaskQ's accepted resolved verdict, allowing only the appended delivery receipt. PilotQ does not replay TaskQ transitions, fetch prose URLs or infer acceptance from a raw trusted comment alone.

The existing finding projection retains Changed, Open and Report; Checks remain evidence rather than conversational detail. Notifications still use the existing subscription, route and durable outbox. No TaskQ write, image, answer, schema, dependency, access or service path changes. Candidate version is 0.2.40 (§9); no release or installation occurs here.

### Verification

`cargo test --locked --bin pilotq closed_publish_331 -- --nocapture`: failure before, pass after. The observer fixture additionally verifies revision drift during retrieval, actual findings in durable delivery, repeated observations and journal restart without another closing effect, and one destination route.

The initial Cargo command encountered an uninitialized specq submodule; initializing the repository's pinned submodule resolved the build input. An initial `--lib` selection ran zero matching tests and is not regression evidence. Candidate checks and affected answer/image protection are recorded in the task result and PR, with their actual outcomes.

## Options

- Consume TaskQ's resolved closed verdict with exact source and revision readback. Gain: one lifecycle authority and existing durable delivery. Cost: bounded additional CLI reads at closure. Selected.
- Reimplement the TaskQ fold in PilotQ. Gain: local resolution. Cost: another acceptance authority and lifecycle drift; rejected (R12, §5).

## Verdict

The defect belongs to PilotQ's closing-result projection. Invented local fixtures establish the parser failure and corrected delivery; they do not qualify installed Telegram or TaskQ lifecycle semantics.

## Recommendation

Accept the scoped candidate through review and required merge-result CI. PM retains final-source A40, release/install and Chrome qualification (§9, R19).
